Soups

Soup is one of the world's oldest and most universal foods, said Janet Clarkson, author of the book 'Soup: A Global History'. 'Every culture has somw kind of soup', she said. 'It's got very ancient roots'. Early people simmered it in everything from turtle shells to lengths of bamboo, she writes in the book, turning out metal soup pots starting in the Bronze Age.

Boiling food made it possible to subsist on stable grains, with herbs and other ingredients or medicinal purpose. Each time you deliver a pot of hearty soup (with perhaps a side of bread) to a friend with the sniffles. Clarkson said you're in fact carrying an age-old tradition. 'Separating food and medicine - that's not how ancient people thought of it', she said. "I think in every country in the world, historically, some soups were seen as restorative."
